# Content Guide — ACaring Adult Home Website

_Last updated: 2026-06-08_

## Core message

ACaring Adult Home should present itself as a warm, responsible, home-based care setting where families and referral partners can start a careful conversation about fit.

## Audience segments

| Audience | What they need |
|---|---|
| Family members | Reassurance, clarity, warmth, next steps |
| Guardians | Trust, professionalism, contact pathway |
| Case managers | Fit, responsiveness, basic services, referral clarity |
| Discharge planners | Contact speed, availability path, care setting clarity |
| WA Cares/network contacts | Provider clarity and service positioning |
| Placement coordinators | Who the home serves, how to refer, contact details |

## Voice

Use:

- “start a conversation”
- “learn whether the home may be a good fit”
- “daily support”
- “home-like setting”
- “family communication”
- “respectful care”
- “clear next steps”

Avoid:

- “guaranteed placement”
- “best care in Washington”
- “we treat/cure/manage everything”
- “submit all medical details here”
- “limited time offer”
- “luxury senior living” if not accurate

## Page-by-page content direction

### Home page

Purpose:

- Calm first impression
- State who the site is for
- Direct visitors to contact/referrals/services

Should include:

- Clear headline
- Short home description
- Primary CTA: Contact / Start the Conversation
- Secondary CTA: Referrals / Who We Serve
- Trust-building sections

### About page

Purpose:

- Explain philosophy and home environment

Should include:

- Why the home exists
- Care approach
- Family communication
- Respect and dignity

Avoid:

- Overly personal backstory if it distracts from trust
- Unsupported credentials

### Care services page

Purpose:

- Explain support categories without overpromising

Should include:

- Daily living support
- Meals
- Supervision
- Medication support only if accurate and authorized
- Coordination/communication
- Dementia/developmental disability support only if accurate and within scope

### Who we serve page

Purpose:

- Help people determine potential fit

Should include:

- General resident profile
- Conditions/support needs only if accurate
- Fit conversation CTA
- “Every placement depends on assessment and fit” language

### Daily life page

Purpose:

- Show rhythm and environment

Should include:

- Morning
- Meals
- Activities
- Rest/quiet time
- Evening
- Family contact

### Events page

Purpose:

- Show activity, routine, community

Should include:

- Evergreen activities
- Upcoming notes if maintained
- Privacy-safe images

### Referrals page

Purpose:

- Serve professional referral partners

Should include:

- Who can refer
- How to start
- What information can be discussed by phone
- Contact CTA

### Contact page

Purpose:

- Convert interest into conversation

Should include:

- Phone
- Email
- Form
- Privacy notice
- Response expectation
- Location/service area

## Claims discipline

Do not make claims that require proof unless proof is provided.

Public HTML should not include internal website-builder language such as instructions to "make the site feel active," "show" a section, or explain why a page exists for marketing purposes. Write directly to families, guardians, referral partners, and community professionals.

Dementia, developmental disability, behavioral health, and mental health language must remain conditional unless licensing, specialty designation, staffing scope, and owner approval are verified. Use phrases such as "inquiries," "support needs," and "fit depends on assessment" instead of implying specialty care.

Contact and referral forms should stay general and privacy-safe. Do not ask visitors to submit detailed medical records, diagnosis details, medication lists, Social Security numbers, Medicaid or Medicare numbers, or other sensitive personal information through the public form.

Placement and services depend on resident assessment, care needs, safety considerations, licensing requirements, specialty designation where applicable, staffing, current availability, payer requirements, and whether the home can safely meet the resident's needs.

Avoid Medicare or Medicaid acceptance claims unless they are verified and carefully qualified. Do not imply guaranteed acceptance, guaranteed placement, skilled nursing, hospital-level care, full-service medical care, or 24/7 nursing care.

Examples:

| Weak/risky claim | Safer alternative |
|---|---|
| “We provide the best dementia care” | “We welcome conversations about dementia-related support needs and fit.” |
| “We guarantee placement” | “Placement depends on assessment, availability, and whether the home is the right fit.” |
| “We handle all medical needs” | “We support daily routines and coordinate communication as appropriate.” |
| “Residents are always happy” | “We aim to provide a calm, respectful, home-like environment.” |
