# Compliance and Reference Notes — ACaring Adult Home Website

_Last updated: 2026-05-30_

This file is not legal advice. It is a practical reference list for future review and implementation discipline.

## Why this matters

An Adult Family Home website sits close to health, care, disability, aging, and resident privacy issues. Even when the site is simple, public-facing content should avoid collecting or exposing sensitive personal information.

## Official references to review

### Washington Adult Family Home references

Washington DSHS lists Adult Family Home provider information and links to relevant laws/regulations, including AFH Minimum Licensing Requirements, training requirements, resident rights, and Adult Family Home statutes.

Reference:

- Washington DSHS — Information for Adult Family Home Providers
- Washington DSHS — Information for AFH Prospective Providers
- Washington DSHS — Residential Care Services

### HIPAA/privacy references

HHS explains that the HIPAA Privacy Rule establishes national standards protecting medical records and other individually identifiable health information.

HHS also notes that covered entities generally need valid written authorization before disclosing protected health information in public-facing contexts such as testimonials, social campaigns, media, photo/video contexts, or similar public uses.

Reference:

- HHS — HIPAA Privacy Rule
- HHS — Summary of HIPAA Privacy Rule
- HHS — Guidance on media/film crew access to PHI
- HHS — HIPAA authorization guidance

## Practical website implications

For this website:

- Do not collect detailed health information through a public form.
- Do not publish identifiable resident images without confirmed authorization.
- Do not use testimonials that reveal health or care details without authorization and review.
- Do not post documents, medication information, resident names, or room-level identifiers.
- Use general inquiry language and move sensitive discussion to an approved private communication channel.
